# Product

## Register

brand

## Users

Two audiences meeting one person:

- **Parents & content followers** — people who found Lalida through her child-development content on TikTok, Instagram, and YouTube (10K+ following). They want research-grounded, calm, practical help decoding their child's behavior. Context: on a phone, often tired, seeking reassurance from someone credible rather than another opinion.
- **Teams, hiring leads, and consulting clients** — product and research leaders evaluating Lalida for UX research leadership, consulting, speaking, or mentoring. Context: skimming for proof of senior impact and range. They need to trust fast.

The site's job is to hold both without diluting either: one coherent person — a child development specialist and research leader — with two clear doors.

## Product Purpose

A personal home base for Lalida Sritanyaratana positioning her as a **UX Research Lead & Child Development Specialist**. It carries the throughline that unifies a decade at Google and her current child-development work: *understanding how people actually behave, then shaping things that fit their lives.* Success = a parent trusts her enough to follow and learn; a hiring lead or client trusts her enough to reach out; and neither audience feels the site was built for the other.

Key repositioning captured here (the previous site is out of date): Lalida has concluded 10+ years at Google (Aug 2015 – Jan 2026) and her current lead role is an independent child-development research and education project (Feb 2026 – present). The Google/Waymo/Stanford record becomes deep credibility, not the headline.

## Brand Personality

Calm authority. Warm, credible, human, precise. The voice of a thoughtful expert you'd trust with something that matters — grounded, never clinical; warm, never cutesy. Three words: **warm, credible, considered.** Emotional goal on load: reassurance backed by evidence.

## Anti-references

- **Cutesy / childish parenting-blog tropes** — no baby pastels, cartoon blocks, crayon fonts, nursery clip-art, or bubbly mascots. Parenting content, grown-up execution.
- **Corporate / clinical** — no sterile SaaS-blue, stock-photo consultant template, or generic agency feel. It must read as a real person.
- **Cold minimalism** — not so stripped-back it feels empty or impersonal; warmth and personality must come through.
- **Loud / overstimulating** — no aggressive animation, heavy gradients, or attention-grabbing effects.
- The site's own prior identity: the neon-cyberpunk theme (magenta/cyan glow, Orbitron, dark bg) is explicitly retired.

## Design Principles

1. **One person, two doors.** Present a single coherent identity, then let each audience self-select. Never make parents wade through case studies or clients wade through parenting content to find their path.
2. **Evidence carries warmth.** Credibility (a billion users, named launches, real testimonials) and reassurance are the same move here — show proof plainly and let it feel human, not boastful.
3. **Practice what she preaches.** A researcher's site should itself be clear, considered, and easy to navigate — the craft is part of the argument.
4. **Grown-up warmth.** Warmth comes from typography, space, tone, and a committed accent — not from softening into a children's aesthetic.
5. **Calm over clever.** Restraint and rhythm over effects. Motion and color are used deliberately and sparingly.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

- Target WCAG 2.1 AA: body text ≥ 4.5:1, large/UI ≥ 3:1, verified against the warm-paper background (muted warm grays must not drift below contrast).
- Full keyboard navigability and visible focus states; semantic landmarks and heading order.
- `prefers-reduced-motion` honored on every animation (crossfade/instant fallback).
- Respect that the parenting audience is frequently mobile and low-attention: legible type sizes, generous tap targets, no motion that blocks reading.
